This project, Salon Experiment & Causal Analysis, tests whether a change launched last quarter by a fictional Coimbatore salon and spa chain really improved results, and designs a proper A/B test for a new initiative. I built a simulated dataset matching the brief (22 outlets, about 312,000 rows across appointments, product sales, stylists and outlets) and cleaned it: no-shows hidden as "Cancelled" were recovered, 37 different service spellings were mapped to 10 services, duplicates were removed, and walk-ins without client IDs were handled as their own segment.

In Part A I designed an A/B test of a WhatsApp reminder with a one-tap reschedule link. I wrote the hypotheses, chose no-show rate as the primary metric, selected four guardrail metrics (opt-outs, revenue per visit, 60-day retention, cancellations), and used power analysis to calculate 7,524 clients per arm (15,048 in total) and a 9-week enrolment period. In Part B I analysed the experiment dataset: there was no sample ratio mismatch, the groups were balanced, and the no-show rate fell from 11.9% to 9.3% (-2.6 percentage points, 95% CI -3.7 to -1.6) with all guardrails passing, so the recommendation is GO.

In Part C I used difference-in-differences against a Tiruppur and Erode control region to measure last quarter's change. A simple before/after is misleading, because the control region moved by a similar amount in either direction. The adjusted effect on revenue per visit is +3.0% (95% CI +0.3% to +5.8%). Parallel trends were tested with a pre-trend test, a placebo launch and an event study, and robustness was checked with bootstrap and permutation tests. Part D shows booked clients gained while walk-in visits may have fallen. As a stretch, CUPED and regression adjustment reduced variance by 1% to 7%, saving up to 0.7 weeks. Every estimate has a confidence interval, assumptions are documented, and the report explains what could make the causal estimates wrong. Deliverables: report, design document, one-page decision memo, and the Python notebook.
